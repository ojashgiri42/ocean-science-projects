This script shows the seabed shear stress and active sediment transport zones off Cape Hatteras where the data was extracted from a HYCOM server. Cape Hatteras was used as it is a high-energy ocean zone where strong currents constantly move sand along the seafloor.
Firstly we get the data from the server, then we isolate the surface model level (depth=0) with u (East-West) and v (North-South) velocities and slice the lat and lon of our proposed region.
We apply the quadratic drag law (tau_b = rho * Cd * speed**2) to the surface current speed and use xr.where to create a binary mask (1 for active transport, 0 for stable sand) where shear stress meets or exceeds the critical threshold (0.18 N/m²).
We use pcolormesh on a 2-panel subplot map to display the shear stress with ('YlOrRd') where low stress is yellow and high stress is dark red, and the transport zone with ('Blues') where stable seabed is white and active erosion is blue.
Finally we lock the color scale between 0 to 0.5 N/m² for stress (vmin=0 and vmax=0.5) and 0 to 1 for mobilization (vmin=0 and vmax=1) to easily spot where sand is actively being moved by currents.
Note: the drag law is applied to surface currents, not near-bed currents, so these values illustrate the method and are not an estimate of actual bed shear stress; no sediment transport is computed. Understanding where currents stress the seabed helps predict coastal erosion, sandbar movement, and the stability of underwater cables or wind turbine foundations.
